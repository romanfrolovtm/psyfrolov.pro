Work drains all my energy — is this burnout or just tiredness?

2026-08-27·4 min read

You come home with no energy for anything. The weekend passes and the sense of recovery never arrives. The question worth asking: is this tiredness that rest will fix, or something else?

The key difference

Ordinary tiredness passes after rest. Burnout does not. That is the main diagnostic marker: if the sense of exhaustion returns within days of a holiday or a long weekend, the issue is not tiredness.

Three signs by the WHO definition

In 2019 the World Health Organization included burnout in ICD-11 as a phenomenon related to working conditions. Three components are identified:

  • Feelings of energy depletion — not resolved by rest.
  • Mental distance from one's job — cynicism, indifference or irritation toward what used to matter.
  • Reduced professional efficacy — despite the same effort, the results are worse.

If two or more of these apply to you, that is a signal to review your workload without waiting for an acute state.

Why a holiday doesn't solve it

Burnout results from a prolonged imbalance between job demands and the resources available to meet them. A holiday temporarily relieves the symptom but does not change the cause: workload, lack of control or support. That is why the state returns after coming back.

What changes the situation

Research shows the most effective interventions work on two levels at once — structural (workload, autonomy, support) and individual. Psychological work helps recognise early signals, revise beliefs such as "I must keep up with everything", and rebuild boundaries.

Can you burn out doing work you love? Yes. Passion is not protective — if anything, highly engaged people often ignore early signals longer than others.

How long does recovery take? It depends on the depth of the state and whether structural factors actually change. Surface-level exhaustion resolves faster than chronic burnout built up over years.
