Depression: A Modern Evidence-Based View and Approach to Care

2026-08-01·3 min read

Depression is one of the most common mental health conditions in the world, and at the same time one of the most thoroughly studied in terms of effective support. It is not a character weakness, nor something one can “simply overcome through willpower”.

How depression differs from low mood

A low mood is a natural response to difficult circumstances. Depression as a clinical condition is characterised by persistence (symptoms lasting at least two weeks), breadth across several areas (mood, energy, sleep, appetite, concentration, self-esteem), and an impact on the ability to function in daily life.

What evidence-based medicine recommends

The UK's NICE guideline (NG222, 2022) offers a clear algorithm: for less severe depression, antidepressants should not be routinely offered as first-line medication (unless that is the person's informed preference); instead, guided self-help, group or individual CBT, and behavioural activation should be offered. For more severe depression, a combination of psychotherapy and medication is considered.

The cognitive model of depression

Aaron Beck described what is known as the cognitive triad of depression — persistent negative beliefs about oneself ("I am worthless"), the world ("everything is meaningless"), and the future ("nothing will change"). Working with these beliefs, alongside behavioural activation, forms the core of the CBT approach to depression.

Depression and insomnia

These conditions often occur together: disrupted sleep worsens depressive symptoms, and depression impairs sleep quality. Systematic reviews suggest that addressing insomnia can also have a positive effect on depressive symptoms.

The key thing to know

Depression is a condition that responds to psychological support with a high degree of proven effectiveness. Seeking help is not a sign of weakness — it is a rational step, backed by decades of clinical research.

Practical steps

One of the practical difficulties of depression is that it drains the very energy needed to address it. That is why it helps to start with minimal, almost automatic actions: a fixed wake-up time, a short walk, one contact with another person a day. These actions do not "cure" depression by themselves, but they create a basic structure that further work — therapeutic or medication-based — can build on. It also matters not to judge yourself by "healthy" productivity standards during this period — expectations should be temporarily lowered, not fought with extra pressure.

Questions people often ask

How does depression differ between people? Symptoms vary: some experience mainly sadness, others apathy and a lack of emotion altogether, others irritability. This is one reason diagnosis is carried out by a specialist rather than a self-checked list of signs.

Can a person recover fully? Yes, for most people a depressive episode resolves. The goal of the work is not only to ease symptoms but also to reduce the risk of recurrence in the future.
